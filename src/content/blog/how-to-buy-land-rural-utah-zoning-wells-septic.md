Buying land in rural Central Utah comes down to four things that determine whether a parcel is actually usable for what you want: zoning, water, septic, and access. Here's what each one means in practice.

## Zoning

Zoning determines what you're legally allowed to build and how the land can be used — residential, agricultural, and recreational zoning all carry different rules. A parcel zoned agricultural, for example, may restrict how many structures you can build or require minimum acreage before you can add a second dwelling. Always confirm current zoning with the county, not just what the listing implies.

## Water Rights

This is the single most misunderstood part of buying land in Utah. Water rights are a separate legal asset from the land itself and are not automatically included in a land purchase. Before you buy, confirm:

- Whether the parcel has documented water rights, and how much
- Whether those rights transfer with the sale or are held separately
- Whether the property has an existing well, and if so, its documented flow rate and condition

A beautiful piece of land with no confirmed water rights can be effectively unbuildable, or far more expensive to develop than expected.

## Septic Requirements

If the parcel isn't on a municipal sewer system — and most rural land in Sanpete, Sevier, Millard, and Juab Counties isn't — you'll need a septic system, which requires a perc test (percolation test) to confirm the soil can support one before you can build. Some parcels fail this test or require an expensive engineered system. Get this confirmed before you're emotionally attached to a piece of land.

## Access

Physical access and legal access are two different things. A road might visibly lead to a parcel, but if it crosses someone else's property without a recorded easement, you may not have a legal right to use it. Always verify:

- Whether access is via a public road or a private easement
- If private, whether the easement is recorded and specifically grants access to your parcel
- Whether the road is maintained, and by whom

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to test for water before buying land in Utah?
Yes, strongly recommended. Confirm documented water rights and, if there's an existing well, its flow rate and condition before purchasing — don't assume water availability from the land's appearance or location.

### What is a perc test and do I need one?
A percolation test measures how well soil absorbs water and determines whether a parcel can support a septic system. If the land isn't on municipal sewer, you'll need a passing perc test before you can build.

### Can I buy land in Utah without confirmed road access?
You can, but it's risky. Always verify whether access is a recorded, legal easement rather than assuming a visible road grants you the right to use it.

### Is agricultural-zoned land the same as residential land in Utah?
No. Agricultural zoning often restricts the number of structures and may require minimum acreage for additional dwellings. Always confirm current zoning with the county before assuming what you can build.
