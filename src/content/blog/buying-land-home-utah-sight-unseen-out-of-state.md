Buying land or a home sight-unseen in Central Utah is common among out-of-state buyers, and it's entirely manageable — but it requires more deliberate verification than a purchase where you can just drive by and look. Here's how the process actually works.

## What Replaces an In-Person Visit

- **Live or recorded video walkthroughs.** I do these for out-of-state buyers regularly, walking the property, the surrounding road, and — for land — the actual parcel boundaries where they're identifiable.
- **A qualified local inspector.** For rural property, this means someone experienced with well systems, septic, and older or manufactured construction, not just a generalist inspector.
- **County records and title work**, which confirm zoning, easements, water rights, and any liens or boundary disputes before you're financially committed.
- **A local agent who will tell you what the camera doesn't show** — road noise, neighboring land use, cell coverage, how the property actually feels in person.

## Land-Specific Verification

Buying vacant land sight-unseen carries extra risk if you skip these steps:

- **Confirm the parcel boundaries** through a survey or clear plat map, not just GPS coordinates from a listing
- **Verify water rights** separately from the land itself — in Utah, water rights are not automatically included with land ownership and require their own documentation
- **Check access legally, not just physically** — a dirt road might look like access on satellite imagery but may be a private easement you don't actually have rights to use

## Financing Considerations

Financing sight-unseen works the same as financing any purchase, but appraisals and inspections become even more important since they're your primary source of truth about the property's actual condition. Land loans in particular usually require a larger down payment and more documentation than a home purchase — plan for that as part of your sight-unseen process, not a surprise at closing.

## Protecting Yourself

- Never waive an inspection contingency on a sight-unseen purchase, regardless of how good the deal looks
- Get a title company involved early, and use one experienced specifically with rural Utah transactions
- Ask for recent, dated photos or video, not just listing photos that could be years old
- Work with an agent who will give you an honest video walkthrough, including the parts that aren't flattering

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is it safe to buy land in Utah without visiting first?
It can be, with the right verification steps — a survey or plat confirmation, water rights documentation, title work, and a video walkthrough from someone you trust. Skipping these steps is where sight-unseen land purchases go wrong.

### Do I need a special inspector for a sight-unseen rural home purchase?
Yes, ideally. A generalist home inspector may not have deep experience with well systems, septic, or manufactured home construction — all common in Central Utah's rural properties.

### Does land in Utah come with water rights automatically?
No. Water rights are a separate legal asset from land ownership in Utah and must be specifically documented and transferred — never assume they're included.
