Yes, you can finance a cabin or second home in Central Utah, but it works differently than financing a primary residence, and the property itself — not just your finances — determines what's possible. Here's what actually applies.

## Conventional Loans Are the Standard Path

Most cabin and second-home purchases go through conventional financing. Lenders generally require:

- A larger down payment than a primary residence — commonly 10-20% or more, depending on the lender and your overall financial picture
- Slightly higher interest rates than a primary-residence loan of the same amount
- The property must be suitable for year-round occupancy, even if you don't plan to live there full-time — a lender won't finance a structure that isn't legally habitable as a residence

## What Doesn't Apply to Second Homes

- **USDA Rural Development loans** are restricted to primary residences only. A property you plan to use as a cabin or vacation home does not qualify, regardless of location.
- **FHA loans** are also generally restricted to primary residences, with very limited exceptions that rarely apply to a straightforward cabin purchase.

## Where It Gets Complicated

Some rural cabin properties in Central Utah have non-standard features that make conventional financing difficult or impossible:

- Off-grid power (solar-only, generator-dependent) without a documented, reliable system
- Non-conforming construction, additions built without permits, or unclear square footage
- Shared or undocumented access roads and water rights
- Well water without recent testing or septic systems without documented inspection

When a property has these issues, buyers often turn to cash purchases, portfolio loans through smaller local or regional lenders, or seller financing. I flag these issues before you get attached to a listing, not after your loan gets denied.

## Manufactured Homes as Cabins

If you're considering a manufactured or modular home as a cabin, financing follows the same permanently-affixed-to-a-foundation-on-owned-land rule as any manufactured home purchase, and lenders will scrutinize this closely for a non-primary-residence purchase.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What down payment do I need to finance a cabin in Utah?
Typically 10-20% or more for conventional financing, though this varies by lender and the specific property's condition and utilities.

### Can I use a USDA or FHA loan for a second home?
No. Both are restricted to primary residences. Cabin and second-home purchases go through conventional financing or, in some cases, cash or portfolio lending.

### Why would a lender refuse to finance a cabin?
Common reasons include off-grid or unreliable power, non-conforming or unpermitted construction, undocumented well or septic systems, or unclear road access — all of which are more common in rural cabin properties than in standard homes.

### Can I get a mortgage on an off-grid cabin?
It depends on the specifics. Some off-grid properties qualify for conventional financing if power and water systems are well-documented and reliable; others require cash or specialized portfolio lending. I evaluate this property-by-property.
