A USDA Rural Development loan lets eligible buyers purchase a primary residence with 0% down in qualifying rural areas — and most of Sanpete, Sevier, and Millard Counties, along with parts of Juab County, qualify. It's one of the most underused financing tools I see, mostly because buyers assume it doesn't apply to them or don't know it exists.

## How USDA Loan Eligibility Actually Works

Two things determine eligibility, and both must be met:

- **Property location.** The property must be in a USDA-designated rural area. Most of my four-county territory qualifies, though eligibility is address-specific — a property just outside Richfield's more populated core, for instance, may or may not qualify depending on exact boundaries.
- **Household income.** USDA loans have income limits based on household size and county, designed to serve moderate-income buyers rather than being open to all income levels.

I check both before you get attached to a property, since either factor alone can disqualify a purchase that otherwise looks perfect on paper.

## What USDA Loans Cover — and What They Don't

USDA loans apply to:
- Primary residences only — not second homes, cabins, or investment properties
- Existing homes and, in many cases, new construction
- Manufactured homes, under specific USDA construction and installation standards

USDA loans do not apply to:
- Second homes or vacation properties, regardless of location
- Multi-family or investment properties
- Properties outside designated rural boundaries, even by a small margin

## Why This Matters for Central Utah Buyers Specifically

Buyers relocating from Salt Lake City or Utah County often assume they need a substantial down payment to make a move like this work. In much of Sanpete, Sevier, Millard, and outlying Juab County, that's simply not true if you and the property both qualify for USDA financing — it can be the difference between renting for another two years and buying now.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do all of Sanpete, Sevier, Millard, and Juab Counties qualify for USDA loans?
Most of the rural areas in all four counties qualify, though eligibility is determined property-by-property based on USDA's designated rural boundaries. More populated cores like Richfield or parts of Nephi may fall outside eligible boundaries — I verify this for each specific property.

### What income qualifies for a USDA loan in Utah?
USDA loans have income limits based on household size and the specific county, targeting moderate-income buyers. Limits vary by county and are updated periodically — I can point you to current figures for your household size and target county.

### Can I use a USDA loan for a manufactured home?
Yes, provided the home meets USDA's specific construction and installation standards. Not every manufactured home qualifies, so this needs to be verified for the specific property.

### Can I use a USDA loan to buy a cabin or second home?
No. USDA loans are restricted to primary residences only, regardless of the property's location or rural qualification.
